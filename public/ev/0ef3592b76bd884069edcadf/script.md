## Segment B — The observed ad and destination (0:12 - 0:25)

> The ad headline reads 'Vancouver's Top-Rated Dentist - 5-Star Rated by 300+ Patients' — a strong promise built on genuine review proof. When someone clicks, they land on the homepage and see a general welcome rather than the top-rated story the ad just told them.

---

## Segment C — One or two meaningful opportunities (0:25 - 1:00)

> First, someone who clicked a top-rated ad is looking for that proof right away — the four-point-nine star rating and the three hundred plus Google reviews you've genuinely earned, plus Dr. Pazhoh's three decades in practice. Right now that story sits further down the page instead of greeting the visitor at the top, so opening with it could help more of those clicks feel confident enough to pick up the phone. Second, right at the moment of action, two phone numbers sit close together — one for new patients, one for existing — and a first-time caller from a paid ad has to pause and figure out which one is theirs. Leading with just the new-patient number on the paid landing experience could quietly remove that small moment of hesitation.

---

## Segment D — Business results and measurement (1:00 - 1:18)

> Getting clicks is only part of the picture. What matters is how many turn into calls or inquiries, then booked appointments, and ultimately patients you actually get paid for. If you can follow that path, you can start seeing which advertising dollars are bringing in real business.
