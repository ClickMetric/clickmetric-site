## Segment A — Opening (0:00 - 0:12)

> I was searching Google for "24 hour dentist vancouver bc" in Vancouver, and I came across one of your paid ads. I spent a few minutes looking at where it sends people, and I noticed a couple of things worth checking.

---

## Segment B — The observed ad and destination (0:12 - 0:25)

> The ad reads 'Emergency Dentist, Vancouver - Walk-In Dental Care Now', and clicking it brings someone to a dedicated emergency page on your website with the emergency phone number right at the top. That matches the promise in the ad well — a lot of clinics send paid clicks to a generic homepage, so starting people on an emergency page is already a stronger foundation than most.

---

## Segment C — One or two meaningful opportunities (0:25 - 1:00)

> The first thing worth flagging is that the top of your emergency page gives a visitor the phone number but no quick way to send a short message. Someone browsing late at night or in the middle of a meeting may not want to call right then, and without a simple form on the page, their only option is to leave and keep looking. The second thing worth a look is that your Google review rating isn't visible when someone first lands on the page. For a patient in pain comparing two or three dental ads side by side, a visible star rating is often the thing that tips the decision toward one clinic.

---

## Segment D — Business results and measurement (1:00 - 1:18)

> Getting clicks is only part of the picture. What matters is how many turn into calls or inquiries, then quotes, and ultimately jobs you actually get paid for. If you can follow that path, you can start seeing which advertising dollars are bringing in real business.

---
