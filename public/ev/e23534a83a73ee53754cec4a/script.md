## Segment A — Opening (0:00 - 0:12)

> I was searching Google for "dentist vancouver bc" in Vancouver, and I came across one of your paid ads. I took a closer look at where that click actually sends people, and noticed a couple of things worth sharing.

---

## Segment B — The observed ad and destination (0:12 - 0:25)

> The ad headline reads 'Vancouver Dental Clinic | Accepting New Patients' — a direct, specific promise to someone searching for a new dentist. When they click it, they land on a general dentistry service page that opens by explaining preventative care, and the specific promise that pulled them in isn't repeated at the top of the page.

---

## Segment C — One or two meaningful opportunities (0:25 - 1:00)

> A new patient clicking that ad expects the destination to confirm in plain terms that the practice is open to new patients and show them how to get started. Right now the top of the page leads with a general dentistry heading and two buttons — Call Now and Book Your Appointment — and the booking button opens a separate page rather than a short form right there on the hero. The second thing worth looking at is that there's no visible review score or star rating in that first screen. For a patient comparing two or three sponsored dental clinics on the same results page, a visible rating is often what tips the choice, and both of these could be addressed in the same stretch of space at the top of the page.

---

## Segment D — Business results and measurement (1:00 - 1:18)

> Getting clicks is only part of the picture. What matters is how many turn into calls or inquiries, then quotes, and ultimately jobs you actually get paid for. If you can follow that path, you can start seeing which advertising dollars are bringing in real business.
