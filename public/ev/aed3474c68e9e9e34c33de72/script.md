## Segment A — Opening (0:00 - 0:12)

> I was searching Google for "vancouver bc pest control" in Vancouver, and I came across one of your paid ads. I took a quick look at where it sends potential customers and noticed a couple of things worth checking.

---

## Segment B — The observed ad and destination (0:12 - 0:25)

> Your ad reads 'Pest Control Vancouver | Single Visits Or Monthly Plans', which is a clear, specific promise for someone searching in Vancouver. When a potential customer clicks it, they land on your main homepage — a general page serving every visitor at once, rather than a page built around that one promise.

---

## Segment C — One or two meaningful opportunities (0:25 - 1:00)

> First, someone who clicks that ad is ready to act on a Vancouver pest problem, but the homepage asks them to navigate menus for rodents, bed bugs, promotions, and resources before they get to a next step — a single-purpose page that mirrors the ad's promise, and puts your 342 five-star Google reviews right under the heading, could meet that visitor where they are. Second, the "Get a free quote" button sends people to a separate contact page with a longer form — service type, pest type, property details, and more — before they can leave their name and number, so a short three-field form right in the hero, with the longer version as a follow-up, may capture the people who otherwise click away.

---

## Segment D — Business results and measurement (1:00 - 1:18)

> Getting clicks is only part of the picture. What matters is how many turn into calls or inquiries, then quotes, and ultimately jobs you actually get paid for. If you can follow that path, you can start seeing which advertising dollars are bringing in real business.

---

## Segment E — ROI and 15-minute invitation (1:18 - 1:35)

> Smart business owners don't mind investing in advertising when they know it's producing a worthwhile return. If it's bringing in profitable jobs, spending more can make sense. What I've shown you is just a couple of things worth looking at. Give us 15 minutes and we can walk through what we found, what else might be worth checking, and how to measure clicks all the way through to paying customers.
