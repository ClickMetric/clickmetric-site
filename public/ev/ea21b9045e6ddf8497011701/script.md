> I was searching Google for "cosmetic dentist vancouver bc" in Vancouver, and I came across one of your paid ads. I took a quick look at where that click actually sends a potential patient, and there are a couple of things I thought were worth flagging.

## Segment B — The observed ad and destination (0:12 - 0:25)

> Your ad showed up right at the top of the paid results with the headline 'Cosmetic Dentistry Vancouver | Smile Makeover Vancouver'. When I clicked it, instead of landing on a page about cosmetic dentistry, I landed on the general practice homepage welcoming new patients — so the specific promise in the ad isn't really continued on the page.

---

## Segment C — One or two meaningful opportunities (0:25 - 1:00)

> The first thing worth looking at is message match. Someone searching for cosmetic dentistry is in a very specific mindset — thinking about veneers, whitening, a smile makeover — and when they land on a general welcome page, they have to hunt around to confirm you even do that work, and some will just bounce. The good news is you already have a dedicated cosmetic page on your site, so pointing the ad at that page is a low-effort change. The second thing is that even that cosmetic page is mostly text — there's no before-and-after smile gallery, and cosmetic dentistry is one of the most visual decisions a patient makes, so adding real patient photos and surfacing the AACD credential you already have could meaningfully lift how many inquiries come from that same ad spend.

---

## Segment D — Business results and measurement (1:00 - 1:18)

> Getting clicks is only part of the picture. What matters is how many turn into calls or inquiries, then consultation bookings, and ultimately paying patients who go ahead with treatment. If you can follow that full path, you can start seeing which advertising dollars are actually bringing real patients through the door.
