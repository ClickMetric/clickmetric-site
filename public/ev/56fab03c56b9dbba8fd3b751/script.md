## Segment B — The observed ad and destination (0:12 - 0:25)

> Your ad reads 'Vancouver Accountants | Trusted Vancouver CPA', and when someone clicks it they arrive on the homepage. That page leads with the firm name and a general tagline, so the specific tax and CPA promise from the ad isn't the first thing a visitor sees when they land.

---

## Segment C — One or two meaningful opportunities (0:25 - 1:00)

> A couple of things stood out. First, when someone clicks an ad about personal and corporate tax, they usually expect the page they land on to open with exactly that — the service they just searched for, right at the top. Right now a visitor has to scroll past the firm name and scan the services grid to confirm they're in the right place, and some people quietly leave before they do. Second, there's nothing on the page that tells a prospect how quickly they'll hear back. When someone is comparing three or four accountants during tax season, the firm that says "replies within one business day" out loud often wins the call, simply because they said it.

---

## Segment D — Business results and measurement (1:00 - 1:18)

> Getting clicks is only part of the picture. What matters is how many turn into calls or inquiries, then quotes, and ultimately jobs you actually get paid for. If you can follow that path, you can start seeing which advertising dollars are bringing in real business.
